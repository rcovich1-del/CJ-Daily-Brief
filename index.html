7 Strategy in Focus

Iron Condor Bias: Neutral Selected for: Caution regime · Monday

An iron condor sells an out-of-the-money put spread and an out-of-the-money call spread on the same underlying and the same expiry. It collects premium from both sides and profits if the underlying finishes between the two short strikes. It is a neutral, defined-risk, positive-theta structure — which is why it is the right fit for the current regime rather than a directional one: the S&P is 0.34% under its 20-day and 0.75% over its 50-day with RSI at 50.31, and the caution regime forbids taking a directional stance on that.

Worked example — in delta and percentage terms

UnderlyingA liquid ETF or large-cap, IV rank ≥ 40%
Expiry30–45 days to expiration
Short put≈ 20 delta (≈ 5–6% below spot on an index ETF)
Long put≈ 8–10 delta (wing ≈ 2–3% wider)
Short call≈ 20 delta (≈ 5–6% above spot)
Long call≈ 8–10 delta (wing ≈ 2–3% wider)
Target credit≈ 25–33% of the total wing width
Max lossWing width minus credit received
Approx. probability of profit≈ 60% at entry, from the 20-delta shorts
HOW IT BEHAVES
  • If the underlying goes nowhere: both spreads decay; the position is at its best around 50% of maximum profit, typically 40–60% of the way to expiry.
  • If implied volatility falls: the position gains independently of price. Selling a high IV rank is the actual edge being harvested.
  • If implied volatility rises: the position loses on a mark-to-market basis even with price unchanged — this is the part that surprises people.
  • Near expiry: gamma rises sharply. A short strike that is 2% away with three days left behaves very differently from one 2% away with thirty days left.

Main risk

An iron condor's maximum loss is several times its maximum gain. Collecting roughly a third of the wing width means a single move through a short strike, held to expiry, can wipe out three or more winning positions. That asymmetry is the whole trade: it is a high-probability, low-payoff structure, and it only works if the losses are actually capped at the wing — which requires the long options to be there and to be liquid enough to close. The specific hazard this week is an event, not a drift. Wednesday's FOMC carries an 85.6% market-implied probability of a rate hike, the entire Treasury curve is at 52-week highs, and Monday brings an unnamed bank sanction announcement. A condor opened Monday has both of those inside its life. Implied volatility is likely to expand into Wednesday rather than decay, which works against the position even if price does nothing — and a hawkish surprise is exactly the kind of move that goes through one wing in a session. Sizing the position so that a full maximum loss is tolerable, and accepting that the structure may be underwater before it is profitable, is not optional here.

9 Bottom Line

Friday delivered a real bounce — every major index up, nine of eleven sectors green, a four-day losing streak snapped — on the back of an in-line CPI print and a 3.4% retreat in crude. But the bounce did not repair the internals. Barely a quarter of the S&P 500 trades above its 20-day average, new one-month lows outnumber new highs three-to-one on both exchanges, credit is deeply oversold and unconfirming, and both SPY and QQQ remain stuck beneath their 20-day lines with RSI on 50. The trend is intact — 50- and 200-day averages hold, both indices sit in the top fifth of their 52-week ranges — but momentum has stalled. That is the caution regime by definition, and the more severe reading governs.

The week's shape is dictated by two dated events rather than by earnings: an unnamed bank sanction Monday and an FOMC decision Wednesday that markets price at 85.6% for a rate increase, with the whole Treasury curve already at 52-week highs. Earnings are almost irrelevant — Lennar on Wednesday is the only report of consequence, and it will be read as a rates datapoint more than a homebuilder one. Energy is the sole sector positive on both the day and the week, and the energy complex is also where implied volatility is richest relative to its own history.

Whether SPY can close back above its 20-day average at 766.88 before Wednesday's FOMC — and whether it holds the 50-day at 758.62 if it cannot. Those 8 points separate a stalled uptrend from a defense-regime downtrend, and the Fed decision sits squarely in the middle of the question.
