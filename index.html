7. Strategy in Focus

🔷 Featured: Iron Condor — Regime-Appropriate Neutral Income

Why now: The CAUTION regime (VIX 15.20, SPY below 20D but above 50D) signals range-bound, two-sided risk — not a clear directional trend. An iron condor is a premium-collection strategy that profits when the underlying stays within a defined range. With VIX in moderate territory and breadth weak, this is preferable to directional debit or naked premium selling.

How it works: An iron condor combines a short call spread (sell OTM call, buy higher-strike call) with a short put spread (sell OTM put, buy lower-strike put), creating a profit zone in the middle. Maximum profit is realized if the underlying expires between the two short strikes. Maximum loss is the width of one spread minus net premium received.

Structure example (conceptual — adjust to your underlying):
Underlying: A liquid index ETF (e.g., SPY) or high-IV individual name
Sell ~0.20 delta call (roughly +4–5% OTM) | Buy ~0.10 delta call (further OTM for hedge)
Sell ~0.20 delta put (roughly -4–5% OTM) | Buy ~0.10 delta put (further OTM for hedge)
Target: 25–35% of max profit as exit; close at 200% loss on either spread
Days-to-expiration: 20–35 days (theta decay accelerates in final 3–4 weeks)

Example in delta/% terms:
If SPY = $765, a 20-delta condor places the short strikes approximately ±3.5–4% away (~$735 put / ~$795 call). Net premium might be ~$3.00 per spread pair per 100-share lot. Max profit = $300; max loss = width of spread minus $300. Break-even at expiry: $735–$300/100 down or $795+$300/100 up.
Best candidates: Stocks or ETFs where IV Rank ≥ 40%, no earnings within 21 days, options volume >1,000/day, price $20–$500. Current regime filters out any strongly directional setups.
⚠️ Main Risk: A breakout move — either sharply higher (surprise bullish catalyst like a massive AVGO beat) or sharply lower (geopolitical escalation, yield spike) — can breach one of your short strikes and create losses larger than the premium collected. The CAUTION regime makes range-bound outcomes more likely, but low-probability tail events are exactly what iron condors lose money on. Always define your max loss at entry and honor your stop.
