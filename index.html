7Strategy in Focus — The Bull Put Spread

Why this one, today. The regime is Caution: VIX at 15.44 is comfortably below the 18 threshold, but SPY still closed 0.23% under its 20-day average even after gaining 1.13%, while holding its 50-day. That is a tape that has stopped falling without yet establishing that it is rising. A bull put spread is the structure that fits that description precisely, because it does not require the market to go up — it requires the market not to go down by a specified amount, which is a materially weaker claim and the only one this table supports. It is also defined-risk on the side where the risk actually is. The caveat is in Section 4 and belongs here: volatility just fell 12.8% in one session, so the premium being sold tonight is cheaper than it was last night.

How it is built

A bull put spread is one credit spread placed below the market: a short put, and a long put further below it that caps the loss. Two legs, defined risk, and a credit received at entry.

LegActionTypical deltaApprox. distance from spot
Short putSell≈ 15–20−5% to −8%
Long put (lower leg)Buy≈ 8–10−8% to −11%

Worked example — in percentages, not strikes

Take a hypothetical $100 stock with an implied-volatility rank around 60% and 45 days to expiration. Sell the roughly 18-delta put about 6% below spot and buy a further put another 3% below that — a width between the strikes of about 3% of spot, or $3. Suppose the net credit is $0.90 on that $3.00 width. Then: maximum profit $0.90 (the credit, kept if price finishes above the short strike), maximum loss $2.10 (the width minus the credit), a breakeven roughly 6.9% below spot, and a credit worth 30% of the width. The roughly 18-delta short strike implies about an 82% chance that price finishes above it at expiration, before anything is done to the position.

The main risk — read this part twice

The payoff is asymmetric against you, and the direction of that asymmetry is the whole trade. In the example above you risk $2.10 to make $0.90 — 2.3× as much lost as won, which means being right roughly 70% of the time simply breaks even before commissions. A structure that wins most months is not the same as a structure that makes money, and a single trade carried to maximum loss erases more than two winners. The specific danger is a gap: the worst case is a large overnight move straight through the short strike, with no opportunity in between to adjust or close. That is precisely why the earnings filter exists in Section 6, and why elevated-IV-rank names are preferred — the point is to be paid for the gap risk being accepted, not to sell cheap options on a quiet name. Tonight adds a second, more specific hazard. This structure is short volatility as well as short downside, and the volatility it would be sold into has just fallen 12.8% in a single session; if implied volatility expands again after entry, the position loses money even with price sitting perfectly still above the short strike. Size on maximum loss, not on credit received, and decide the exit before entering, not after the gap.
