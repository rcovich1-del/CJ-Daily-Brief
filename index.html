07 Strategy in Focus Regime NORMAL · Tuesday → credit, bullish bias

Bull Put Spread — a defined-risk way to be bullish and get paid to wait

With the regime NORMAL and the tape firmly risk-on, a bull put spread expresses a bullish-to-neutral view while capping risk and collecting premium. You sell a put below the market and buy a further-out-of-the-money put as protection, in the same expiry.

Worked example (delta / % terms): on a liquid, up-trending underlying with IV Rank ≥ 40%, sell the ~30-delta put and buy the ~15-delta put in a 30–45 DTE cycle. A typical net credit runs ~30–35% of the strike width (e.g. collect ~$1.15 on a $3-wide spread). Max profit = the credit, kept in full if the underlying finishes above the short strike; breakeven = short strike − credit; the position is mildly long (net ≈ +0.15 delta) with positive theta working for you each day.

Main risk: the trade's maximum loss (strike width − credit) is several times the credit collected, and it is realized on a move through the long strike. A sharp reversal, an IV spike above VIX ~22, or SPY slipping back under its 50-day at 745.89 can turn the spread against you quickly, and rising IV inflates the short put you are short. Size each spread so its worst case is a small, pre-defined fraction of the account, and avoid names with earnings inside the trade window — which is exactly why most 100%-IV-rank screener hits sit on the monitoring list, not the setup list.
