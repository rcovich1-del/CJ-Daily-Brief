7Strategy in Focus — Monday & Caution Regime
◊ Iron Condor
Neutral, defined-risk credit strategy — ideal for range-bound markets with elevated IV and unclear directional bias. Monday + Caution regime.

An iron condor is a four-leg options position that profits when an underlying stays within a defined range through expiration. It combines a bull put spread (below the market) and a bear call spread (above the market), creating a zone of maximum profit between the two short strikes. The trade collects a net credit upfront and keeps it if the stock stays in the range.

Example structure (SPY at 767):

Sell a 30-delta put at approximately 750 (≈ -2.2% OTM)
Buy a 15-delta put at approximately 740 (≈ -3.5% OTM, lower wing)
Sell a 30-delta call at approximately 785 (≈ +2.3% OTM)
Buy a 15-delta call at approximately 795 (≈ +3.7% OTM, upper wing)

Net credit: If each spread collects $1.50, total credit = $3.00 per condor
Max loss: $10.00 – $3.00 = $7.00 per condor (wing width minus credit)
Max profit: $3.00 (if SPY closes between 750 and 785 at expiration)
Break-evens: 750 – 3.00 = 747 on downside / 785 + 3.00 = 788 on upside

In percentage and delta terms: With 30-delta short strikes, you're implying roughly 70% probability of keeping the full credit. The put spread is positioned about -2.2% below current price and the call spread about +2.3% above — a 4.5% total profit zone. This is appropriate for a caution regime where you expect a range-bound market (which today's mixed signals — VIX low but breadth deteriorating — support). You're not predicting direction; you're selling the uncertainty itself.

Exit rules: Consider closing when you've captured 50% of the max profit (target: $1.50 collected). Also consider closing if the underlying touches a short strike — don't ride a loser through expiration.

⚠ Main risk: An iron condor loses on large directional moves that push past your short strike. Today's geopolitical context (Iran/Strait of Hormuz) is precisely the type of binary event that can generate gap moves — if a new escalation pushes oil above $90 and stocks gap down 3%+, your put spread could come under pressure. In a caution regime, position sizing is critical: limit each iron condor to no more than 2–3% of portfolio risk. Widen the wings sufficiently to survive a 3–4% swing without being tested. The September earnings calendar (DELL, PANW report tomorrow) also creates vol-spike risk in individual names. Use iron condors on indexes (SPX, SPY) rather than individual names when geopolitical uncertainty is elevated — indexes have more liquidity and smoother moves.
