7Strategy in Focuschosen by regime (normal) + weekday (Monday)

Bull Put Spread (credit put spread)

A neutral-to-bullish, defined-risk income trade that fits a calm "normal" regime: VIX at 15.99, SPY holding just above its 20- and 50-day. You sell an out-of-the-money put and buy a further-OOTM put to cap risk, collecting a net credit up front.

Worked example (delta / % terms)

On a ~$100 stock in an uptrend, sell the ~25-delta put (roughly 5–7% below spot) and buy the ~12-delta put one strike-width lower, 30–45 days out. Target a net credit near one-third of the spread width (e.g. collect ~$0.33 on a $1-wide spread). Max profit = the credit if the stock stays above the short strike; the ~25-delta short implies roughly a 70–75% probability of finishing OTM. Manage by closing at ~50% of max credit or rolling if the short delta pushes past ~40.

Main risk

The maximum loss is the spread width minus the credit received, and it arrives fast if the stock gaps below the long strike — a downside gap (bad earnings, a macro shock, a jobs-Friday miss) can hand you most of the max loss overnight, and the payoff is asymmetric: you risk far more than you collect. Selling into today's compressed VIX means thin premium for that risk, so position size and strike distance matter more than usual. Defined risk is not small risk.
