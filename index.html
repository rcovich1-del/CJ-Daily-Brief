7Strategy in Focus

Bull Put Spread Normal regime · Thursday

A defined-risk, neutral-to-bullish income structure — appropriate for a normal regime where the trend is up but you want a cushion and a capped downside. You sell a put and buy a further-OTM put below it, collecting a net credit.

Worked example (delta/percentage terms). On a $100 stock with IV Rank ~45% and ~30–45 days to expiry:
  • Sell the ~30-delta put (strike roughly 7% below spot) and buy the ~15-delta put (roughly 12% below spot) — about a $5-wide spread.
  • Collect a credit near 30–35% of the width (~$1.50 on the $5 spread).
  • Net position delta is modestly positive (~+15 to +20); theta works in your favor as time passes.
  • Max profit = the credit, kept if the stock stays above the short strike at expiry. Breakeven = short strike minus credit. Max loss = width minus credit (~$3.50).
Main risk. A fast, deep move below the short strike drives the position toward its max loss — width minus credit, which here is more than twice the credit collected — and near expiry an in-the-money short put carries assignment risk. Critically, in today's low-VIX (15) / low-IV-rank environment the credit is thin, so you are paid less to shoulder the same downside: the risk/reward is worse than in higher-IV regimes. Size so the defined max loss is a small fraction of the account and consider closing at ~50% of max profit rather than holding to expiry.
