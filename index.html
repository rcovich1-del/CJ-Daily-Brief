7 Strategy in Focus — Bear Call Spread

Chosen for the regime (defense) and weekday (Monday). A bear call spread is a defined-risk credit strategy that profits when a stock or index stays below a chosen level — aligned with a market trading under its 20- and 50-day moving averages, with no bullish tilt.

How it works (delta / % terms)

On an index ETF trading near overhead resistance, sell a call around the 25–30Δ strike (roughly 2–3% above spot, near the declining 50-day) and buy a call around the 10–15Δ strike a further 1–2% higher as the defined-risk cap. You collect a net credit; maximum gain is that credit if price stays below the short strike at expiration (≈30–45 days out). Because it is a credit spread, elevated implied volatility improves the entry.

Main risk

The position loses if the underlying rallies through the short call — for example, a swift reclaim of the 50-day on a broad risk-on reversal or a dovish macro surprise. Maximum loss is the spread width minus the credit received, realized if price closes at or above the long strike. The trade-off for defined risk is a capped, modest credit against a larger potential loss, so position sizing and a plan to exit (e.g. if the short strike is breached or the underlying reclaims its 50-day with conviction) matter more than being "right" on direction.
