7 Strategy in Focus

Defense · Bear Call Spread

Selling a defined-risk credit above the market

With the index under its 50-day and a wall of megacap earnings plus the Fed ahead, a bear call spread expresses a neutral-to-lower view while capping risk — the right shape when you want to sell elevated volatility but refuse open-ended upside exposure into binary events.

Example (delta / % terms): sell a ≈30-delta call roughly 3–5% above spot, and buy a ≈15-delta call further out as a hard cap. Target a net credit around 25–35% of the strike width. Max profit is the full credit if the underlying finishes below the short call; breakeven sits at the short strike plus the credit received.

Main risk: a sharp rally — a short squeeze, a dovish Fed, or a blowout MSFT/AMZN print — pushes the underlying through the short call, and losses accelerate until they hit the capped maximum (spread width minus credit received). Early assignment is possible if the short call goes deep in-the-money or around an ex-dividend date. Because the position is short gamma, an adverse move hurts fastest near expiration; size it so the defined max loss is one you can absorb, and treat next week's events as the primary threat to the trade.
