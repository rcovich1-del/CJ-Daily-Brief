Cash-Secured Put (CSP) — Educational Example
A Cash-Secured Put obligates you to buy 100 shares at the strike price if the stock falls to or below that level by expiration. In exchange, you collect premium upfront. The collateral requirement = strike × 100.
Example in Delta / Percentage Terms (not a recommendation)

Stock: XYZ at $100  ·  IV Rank: 55%  ·  Target: 30-delta put
Strike chosen: ~5% below spot = $95  ·  Bid: $2.50
Breakeven: $92.50 (7.5% below spot)
Potential return: $2.50 / $95 = 2.6% in 30 days = 31% annualized
Collateral: $9,500 (secured in cash or T-Bills)

Best outcome: Stock stays above $95 at expiration → full $250 premium kept.
Assignment: Stock falls below $95 → you own 100 shares at $95 (your adjusted cost is $92.50 after premium).

⚠ Main Risk

The primary risk is owning a stock you did not want at a price above fair value. A stock that gaps down 20–30% (earnings miss, fraud, macro shock) means you are immediately underwater even after the premium buffer. Never sell a CSP on a stock you are unwilling to own at the strike price. Size positions so that assignment would represent no more than 3–5% of total portfolio capital in any single name. In a normal regime, limit total CSP exposure to 15–20% of total capital across all positions.
