7Strategy in Focus — Bull Put Spread

In a normal / constructive regime — VIX low, benchmarks above their moving averages, breadth expanding — a defined-risk bullish credit spread fits the tape: it profits from the market simply not falling, collects premium up front, and caps the loss.

Worked example (delta / percentage terms): on an underlying near $250 with 30–40 days to expiry, sell the put at roughly the 25-delta strike (about 5–6% below spot) and buy a protective put near the 10-delta strike a few percent lower. That short delta implies roughly a 70–75% chance the short strike expires worthless. A typical credit runs about 25–35% of the spread width — e.g. collect ~$1.30 on a $5-wide spread, with max risk ~$3.70 if the trade goes fully against you. Manage by taking profit around 50% of max credit and rolling or closing if the short strike is breached.

Main risk: the payoff is asymmetric — you risk multiples of the credit to earn it. A fast gap below the long strike delivers the full defined loss at once, and in a low-VIX environment you are being paid relatively little to accept that tail. If the regime flips (VIX spikes above ~22 or the index loses its 50-day), bullish credit spreads are the first structures to reassess or size down.
