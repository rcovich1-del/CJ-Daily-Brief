7Strategy in Focus

Iron Condor Neutral · defined-risk

Selected by regime (Defense) + weekday (Thursday)

With the index below its 50-day, breadth negative and VIX in the moderate zone, a market-neutral premium-selling structure fits better than any directional bet. The iron condor sells an out-of-the-money call spread and an out-of-the-money put spread simultaneously, collecting a credit that it keeps if price stays range-bound — no bullish featured strategy is permitted while the regime is Defense.

Educational example (delta / % terms): On a ~$120 underlying carrying ~40% implied vol with ~35 DTE, sell the ~16-delta call (≈ +3.5% out-of-the-money) and the ~16-delta put (≈ −3.5% out-of-the-money), then buy ~5-delta wings a few percent further out on each side. The net credit typically runs ~20–25% of the wing width. Because a 16-delta short strike sits roughly one standard deviation away, the position has an ~65–70% probability of finishing between the shorts. Breakevens are the short strikes ± the credit received; max profit is the full credit if price expires inside the short strikes.

Main risk. The iron condor's maximum loss (wing width minus credit) is several times its maximum gain, so one large move — a gap through a short strike on an earnings surprise or a macro shock — can erase many winning trades at once. In a Defense regime with negative breadth and live event risk (AAPL and AMZN tonight, a heavy mega-cap slate Friday), a sharp directional break is the primary threat. Size small, keep the wings defined rather than trading naked strangles, and never place a condor on a name with earnings inside the trade window.
