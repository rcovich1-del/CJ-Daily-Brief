7 Strategy in Focus — The Iron Condor

Selected for a caution regime on a Monday: a neutral, defined-risk, premium-collecting structure that matches a market with no directional edge and a heavy event calendar three days out.

Why this structure, this week

An iron condor sells an out-of-the-money put spread and an out-of-the-money call spread on the same underlying and expiry. The trader collects two credits and profits if the stock finishes between the two short strikes at expiry. Maximum loss is the width of the wider spread minus the total credit received, and it is known before entry.

The regime justifies it. With SPY fractionally under its 20-day, QQQ under both its 20- and 50-day, VIX at 15.85 and the risk-on/caution tally split 5–5, there is no defensible directional bias at the index level. Meanwhile single-name IV ranks sit in the 45–70% band even as index volatility is subdued — a dispersion that pays for two-sided premium in individual names far better than it pays in the indices themselves.

Worked example — delta and percentage terms only

Take a $100 stock with an IV rank near 60% and no earnings inside the expiry cycle. A representative 35-day condor:

Sell put~16 delta — roughly 8% below spot
Buy put~7 delta — roughly 13% below spot
Sell call~16 delta — roughly 8% above spot
Buy call~7 delta — roughly 13% above spot
Wing width~5% of spot on each side
Target credit~30% of the wing width
Max losswing width less credit — ~70% of width
Approx. probability of profit~65–70% at entry
Common managementclose at ~50% of max credit, or at ~21 DTE

Note the asymmetry the numbers describe: this structure wins often and small, and loses rarely and large. The delta selection sets that ratio — wider short strikes raise the win rate and shrink the credit; tighter ones do the reverse.

The main risk

The iron condor's defining danger is that its payoff profile inverts its psychology. A roughly 65–70% win rate feels like a reliable income stream, and for many consecutive cycles it behaves like one — which is exactly what makes traders increase size. But maximum loss is typically two to three times the maximum gain, so a single expiry that closes outside a wing can erase four, five or six winning cycles. That risk is not evenly distributed: it clusters. Losses arrive when volatility expands, and volatility expands across an entire portfolio at once, so multiple condors placed in correlated underlyings — the ten precious-metals names flagged in section 6, for instance — will fail together rather than independently. A second, subtler hazard is gap risk: the structure is defined-risk at expiry, but an overnight move straight through a short strike leaves the position at or near maximum loss with no opportunity to adjust. That is precisely why the earnings-clearance filter exists, and why entering a condor in any name reporting this week — Nvidia on Wednesday most of all — converts a probability trade into a coin flip with terrible odds attached.

9 Bottom Line

Monday was a narrow, low-conviction session that looked flatter than it was. The headline damage was modest — SPY −0.29%, QQQ −1.00%, DIA actually higher at +0.27% — but the composition was defensive throughout: staples, financials and utilities led, technology was the worst of the eleven sectors for the day and the week, and Nasdaq issues closed 626 net decliners while the NYSE line was essentially flat. It happened on 69% of SPY's average volume, which argues this was an absence of buyers rather than an arrival of sellers.

That puts the market in a genuine caution regime and not a worse one. VIX at 15.85 is unremarkable, the term structure is properly upward-sloping from a 14.07 nine-day out to a 21.04 six-month, and SPY remains 1.5% above its 50-day and 7.8% above its 200-day. What has broken is short-term momentum, not trend: SPY is a rounding error beneath its 20-day and QQQ has lost both its 20- and 50-day averages. With three scheduled catalysts — Nvidia Wednesday, Jackson Hole Thursday and Friday — landing inside four sessions, the honest position is that the market has no directional information yet and is waiting for it. Neutral, defined-risk, event-cleared structures are what that environment rewards; directional conviction is what it punishes.

Top focus for Tuesday: whether SPY can reclaim its 20-day moving average at 763.55 on better volume than today's 0.69×. That single line separates a market that is consolidating from one that is rolling over, and it will be settled before Nvidia ever reports.
