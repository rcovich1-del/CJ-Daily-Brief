7. Strategy in Focus — Iron Condor (Caution Regime · Tuesday)
⚙ Iron Condor — Neutral Range-Bound Premium Collection

An Iron Condor is a four-legged options structure that profits when the underlying stays within a defined range by expiration. It's composed of a bull put spread (sell a put, buy a lower put) combined with a bear call spread (sell a call, buy a higher call). Maximum profit is the net premium collected, achieved when the stock closes between the two short strikes at expiration. Maximum loss is capped at the width of either spread minus the credit received.


Why this regime favors the Iron Condor: VIX at 16.34 is moderate — IV is elevated enough to collect meaningful premium, but not so extreme that wide expected moves make short-strike selection difficult. SPY's RSI (48.6) and QQQ RSI (46.6) are in neutral territory — neither stretched overbought nor oversold. The geopolitical shock is real but contained (not a VIX 25+ panic), and the 50-day MA support (754.71 on SPY) provides a known floor. This combination of elevated IV with defined support/resistance makes a condor the preferred neutral structure.

Illustrative Example — SPY 30-DTE Iron Condor (educational, not a recommendation)

• Sell the ~0.20 delta put (approx. 7–8% below current price ~$700–703 area) — collects premium
• Buy the ~0.10 delta lower put (~5 points further down) — limits downside risk
• Sell the ~0.20 delta call (approx. 5–6% above current price ~$805–815 area) — collects premium
• Buy the ~0.10 delta upper call (~5 points further up) — limits upside risk

Net credit received: typically 25–40% of the wing width. Breakeven points sit roughly at the short strikes minus/plus the net credit. The trade profits fully if SPY stays between ~$703 and ~$808 through expiration — a range that encompasses the 50-day MA support and recent resistance.

Entry guidelines: Enter when IV Rank ≥ 40% and at least 21–45 days to expiration (allows time decay to work). Collect ≥ 1/3 the wing width as credit. Short strikes at the 0.15–0.25 delta level. Close the entire structure at 50% of max profit (don't get greedy — the tail risk is real).

Main Risk: An Iron Condor's worst enemy is a large directional move — exactly what the Iran conflict or a surprise Fed statement could deliver. If SPY breaches the 50-day MA (754.71) decisively, the short put side comes under pressure rapidly. The position can also be hurt by IV expansion (a VIX spike above 20–22) even if price hasn't moved to the strike — mark-to-market loss forces early exit at a worse price. Always define your exit rule before entering: a common rule is to exit (take the loss) if the short strike's delta reaches 0.35–0.40, or if the spread reaches 2× the credit received.
