7 Strategy in Focus — Bull Put Spread

Chosen for a Normal regime on a Wednesday: a defined-risk, mildly bullish credit structure that profits from time decay while the trend stays intact. A bull put spread sells a nearer-the-money put and buys a further out-of-the-money put in the same expiration, collecting a net credit.

Worked example (delta / % terms): On a $70 stock, sell the ~30-delta put roughly 6% below spot and buy the ~15-delta put roughly 11% below spot, ~35 days out. If the spread width is $5 you might collect about $1.60 credit. Max profit = the credit (≈$160/contract) kept if the stock finishes above the short put; max loss = width − credit (≈$340) if it closes below the long put. Breakeven sits at the short strike minus the credit. The position wins if the stock rises, stalls, or drifts only modestly lower — positive theta does the work.

Main risk: the payoff is defined but asymmetric — you risk more than twice the credit to earn it. A sharp gap below the long strike (bad earnings, a macro shock) delivers the full max loss at once, and a short put that goes in-the-money near expiry carries assignment risk. The setup assumes the regime holds; a VIX spike above 22 or SPY closing below its 50-day (746.37) would pressure the trade and argue for tighter sizing or an earlier exit.
