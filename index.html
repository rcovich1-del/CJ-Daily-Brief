7 Strategy in Focus — Bull Put Spread

Chosen for a Normal regime (uptrend, moderate IV) on a Tuesday: a defined-risk, bullish-to-neutral credit structure that profits from time decay while the trend holds.

Educational example (delta / % terms): on a $100 stock in an uptrend, sell the ~30Δ put (roughly 5% out-of-the-money) and buy the ~12Δ put (roughly 10% OTM), 30–45 days to expiry. That collects a credit of about 25–35% of the spread width. Max profit is the full credit if the stock closes above the short strike at expiry; breakeven is the short strike minus the credit received. Works best when IV Rank ≥ 40% (richer credit) and the underlying holds above rising 20- and 50-day moving averages.

Main risk: a sharp decline below the long strike realizes the full spread width minus the credit — and losses accrue faster than theta can offset them. Because the position is short an out-of-the-money put, an overnight gap (an earnings miss, a CPI shock, a geopolitical headline) can blow straight through the intended management point, so the maximum loss should be sized as money you are prepared to lose, not as a level you assume you can defend.
