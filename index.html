7 · Strategy in Focus — Cash-Secured Put

Cash-Secured Put (Normal Regime · Tuesday Sept 1 Session)

Why now: VIX at 14.35 is at multi-year lows — this is the low end of what's typically favorable for selling premium. In a normal regime, volatility is low and trending lower, making premium selling strategies like cash-secured puts attractive when IV Rank ≥40% ensures you're selling at a relative high point in that name's volatility. The key is picking a name where IV Rank is elevated even as VIX is low — these are stocks with company-specific catalysts inflating their individual IV above the market average.

How it works (delta and percentage terms): A cash-secured put means selling a single put option on a stock you'd be willing to own, with enough cash reserved to buy 100 shares at the strike price. Aim for the 25-35 delta put strike — roughly 5-10% below the current stock price. This gives approximately a 65-75% probability (100 minus delta) that the option expires worthless. For NKE at $39.60, a 30-delta put might be the $37 strike. If NKE closes above $37 at expiry, you keep the entire premium (profit). If it falls below $37, you buy 100 shares at $37 — effectively a 6.6% discount to Friday's close. Premium received on a 30-day option might be $0.60-$0.80, representing ~1.5-2% of the strike price annualized to ~18-24% return if repeated monthly.

IV Rank matters: NKE's IV Rank of 43.8% means its IV is near the upper 44th percentile of its 1-year range. You're selling premium when NKE's implied vol is near the higher end of what it's been — that's the edge. Avoid selling puts when IV Rank is <30% (selling cheap premium).

Main Risk: The cash-secured put's maximum loss is substantial — if the underlying stock falls sharply (a poor earnings report, sector collapse, or macro shock), you are obligated to buy 100 shares at the strike, which could be well above the new market price. For NKE, which is already in a multi-year downtrend (200D MA is $52 vs current $39.60 — the stock has already fallen 24% from its 200D), the risk of an accelerating decline is real. Never sell a cash-secured put on a stock you would not be comfortable owning at the strike price for an extended period. Size to a maximum of 3-5% of your total portfolio per position, and set a stop-loss rule (e.g., if the stock falls 10% below the strike, close the position regardless of time remaining).
