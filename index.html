7 Strategy in Focus

Chosen by regime (normal) + weekday (Monday) — a defined-risk bullish credit strategy
BULL PUT SPREAD (credit)

Get paid to be patiently bullish

In a normal regime — VIX in the mid-teens, index above its 20-day, trend intact — a bull put spread lets you collect premium while the market drifts, sideways or up. You sell an out-of-the-money put and buy a further-OTM put as a hard floor, so both maximum profit and maximum loss are known before you enter.

Illustrative example (percentages/deltas, not a recommendation):
Stock trading at $100, ~35 days to expiry.
• Sell the ~30-delta put (≈ $95 strike) → collect premium.
• Buy the ~15-delta put (≈ $90 strike) → define risk.
• Net credit ≈ $1.65 on a $5-wide spread → you keep the credit if the stock holds above $95 (~30% probability-ITM short leg means ~70% base odds of full profit).
• Max gain = credit ($165/contract). Max loss = width − credit ($335). Break-even ≈ $93.35.
Main risk: the position is short downside. A sharp gap below the long strike delivers the full max loss ($335 here) regardless of how far price falls — the loss is capped but can still be roughly 2× the credit collected. The strategy also carries negative gamma: a fast selloff hurts faster than a slow grind higher helps. Size positions so a max-loss outcome is survivable, avoid names with earnings inside the spread's life, and consider closing at ~50% of max profit rather than holding to expiry.
