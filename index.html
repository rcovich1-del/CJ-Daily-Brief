7Strategy in Focus

Featured · Bull Put Spread

Chosen for a NORMAL regime on a Monday: a defined-risk credit strategy with a neutral-to-bullish lean, which fits a tape that's above its 20- and 50-day with VIX in the low-moderate zone. Because index vol is compressed, the edge comes from selling into elevated single-name IV rank rather than the broad market.

Educational example (delta / % terms)
  • Pick a liquid underlying with IV Rank ≥40% and no earnings inside the cycle.
  • Sell the ~30-delta put; buy the ~15-delta put below it — a defined-width spread.
  • Target ~30–45 DTE and a credit near ⅓ of the strike width.
  • Short strike sits ~5–8% below spot for a downside buffer; manage at ~50% of max profit or roll near 21 DTE.
Main risk

Max loss is defined (width minus credit) but is realized on a sharp gap down through the short strike — and a low-VIX backdrop means thinner premium for the same risk, so the reward is modest relative to the tail. A fast volatility spike (VIX back above 18–22) can widen the spread against you before the buffer helps. Assignment / early-exercise risk rises if the short leg goes deep ITM near expiry. Size positions so a full-width loss is survivable, and avoid names reporting earnings inside the trade window.
