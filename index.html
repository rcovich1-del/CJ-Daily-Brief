07 Strategy in Focus

Bull Put Spread
Bias: bullish-to-neutral Credit structure Selected for: normal regime · start of week

A normal regime — VIX at 15.13, SPY above both its 20- and 50-day — permits a bullish bias. A Monday open ahead of a Wednesday event cluster argues for a structure that profits from time and volatility decay rather than one that needs a directional move to arrive on schedule. The bull put spread does both: it is defined-risk, it wins on sideways price action, and it is the natural expression when the credit screens are full of high-IV-rank names in intact uptrends.

Educational example — in delta and percentage terms
  • Underlying: a name with an IV rank above 40%, price above its 20- and 50-day averages, and no earnings for at least 14 days.
  • Short leg: sell the put at roughly 20–25 delta, which typically sits 5–8% below spot at 30–45 days. That delta implies a market-assessed probability of expiring in the money of roughly one in four or five.
  • Long leg: buy the put at roughly 10 delta, usually another 3–5% lower. This is what turns an undefined obligation into a fixed maximum loss.
  • Expiry: 30–45 days. Theta accelerates meaningfully inside 45 days while gamma is still manageable.
  • Credit target: aim for a net credit of about 25–33% of the width between the strikes. Below roughly 20% the reward stops paying for the tail.
  • Management: close at 50% of the maximum credit, or at 21 days to expiry, whichever comes first. Both rules exist to exit before gamma risk grows faster than the remaining premium.
  • Position sizing: risk per position is the width minus the credit. Sized so that the maximum loss on any one spread is a small, pre-decided fraction of the account — not so that the credit looks meaningful.
The main risk

The payoff is defined but deeply asymmetric, and that asymmetry is the whole problem. A spread that collects 30% of its width risks roughly 2.3 times the credit to make one — meaning a single full loser erases a bit more than two clean winners. The structure therefore does not fail gradually; it fails all at once. A gap through the short strike on an overnight headline, a sector-wide break, or a downgrade skips every intermediate price at which you might have managed the trade, and defined risk only guarantees the loss is bounded, not that it is small. Worse, the high IV rank that made the credit attractive in the first place is frequently high because the market has correctly identified that something can go wrong — selling volatility is not the same as volatility being overpriced. Two further hazards compound it: gamma risk rises sharply in the final two weeks, which is why the 21-day exit exists; and the strategy's high win rate is psychologically corrosive, tempting traders to size up after a run of winners precisely when a correlated cluster of positions can lose together. Every name on this week's credit screen is levered to the same two macro factors — a soft dollar and firm crude — so four such spreads at once is closer to one large position than four independent ones.

08 Game Plan

Next session backdrop

Monday carries no first-tier US macro release. Two China ADRs — PDD and XPeng — report before the open and will set the tone for that complex, but the index-level risk is concentrated on Wednesday, when core PCE, the second estimate of Q2 GDP and Nvidia's report all land in the same session, followed by Chair Warsh at Jackson Hole on Thursday. The practical read is that Monday and Tuesday are positioning sessions in a thin late-August tape, and the tape is more likely to drift than to trend. Friday's bounce recovered part of a week that still finished 1.37% lower on SPY, and it did so on 15% below-average volume.

Favor

  • Defined-risk credit in high-IV-rank hard-asset names. GDX, FCX and DINO all pair an IV rank near or above 68% with an intact trend and no earnings inside the window.
  • Shorter durations that expire before or shortly after the event cluster — 30–45 days keeps theta working without carrying gamma into Wednesday.
  • Cheap optionality where implied volatility is genuinely low. COP at a 30.7% implied vol and a 41.2% IV rank is the one debit structure the screen supports.
  • Smaller size than the screen would suggest. Four correlated dollar-and-crude spreads are one macro bet wearing four tickers.

Avoid

  • New long exposure in rate-sensitive sectors. XLU sits 4.84% below its 50-day with RSI at 31.7; oversold is not the same as a bottom while the 30-year is still repricing.
  • Undefined short volatility into Wednesday. Core PCE, the GDP revision and Nvidia in one session is not a chart-based risk you can size for.
  • Non-earnings premium sales in names reporting inside 14 days — A, ADSK, NTAP and DELL all screened well on volatility and all fail on this.
  • Chasing the gold and silver complex on momentum. GLD's RSI is 71.2 and it is 10.5% above its 50-day. Selling volatility into that is defensible; buying delta into it is a late entry.

09 Bottom Line

Friday was a broad, low-conviction bounce: nine of eleven sectors green, the Dow up 0.98%, small caps up 0.77%, and net advancers running roughly ten times their 20-day average on both exchanges — all of it on 15% below-average volume, and none of it enough to repair a week that still cost SPY 1.37%. The regime reads normal on every test that matters: VIX at 15.13 in the moderate zone, the term structure cleanly in contango from 12.58 out to 20.90, and SPY 0.45% above its 20-day and 1.86% above its 50-day. But the internals are softer than the headline. The share of S&P names above their 20-day average has slid to 49.4% from a 60.4% run-rate, both index RSIs sit in the neutral band, and the week's real move was rotation — hard assets and health care up, technology, industrials and utilities down — rather than an index trend. The long end of the Treasury curve is the single force behind most of that dispersion, and it has not settled.

Top focus

QQQ closed at 713.44 — its 50-day average, to the penny — with Nvidia reporting Wednesday. That one level decides whether index leadership repairs itself or whether the rotation into materials, energy and health care becomes the entire market. Everything else on this page is downstream of it.
