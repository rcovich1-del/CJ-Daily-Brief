7.Strategy in Focus — Bear Call Spread

Chosen for a defense regime on a mid-week session with an elevated VIX: a bear call spread (call credit spread) is a defined-risk, premium-collecting structure with a bearish-to-neutral bias — it does not fight the risk-off tape, and the recent IV pop richens the credit.

Educational example (delta / percentage terms):
On an ETF trading at $100 with IV Rank ~45%, sell the ~30-delta call ≈3% OTM ($103) for ~$1.60 and buy the ~15-delta call ≈6% OTM ($106) for ~$0.60, same expiry ~30–45 DTE.
• Net credit ≈ $1.00 on a $3-wide spread → max profit ≈ $100/contract (the credit).
• Max loss ≈ $200 ($3 width − $1 credit).
• Breakeven = short strike + credit = $104 (~4% of cushion above spot).
• Greeks: delta-negative, theta-positive, vega-negative — it profits from time decay and from IV falling back toward normal.
Main risk. Profit is capped at the credit while the loss, though defined, can run to roughly twice the credit. A sharp rally — a dovish Fed surprise, or a blowout mega-cap reaction gapping the tape higher — can carry the underlying through both strikes to the full loss. Because the position is vega-negative, an IV spike arriving alongside an up-move worsens the mark-to-market before expiry. Size so the max loss is a small fraction of capital, and plan to close or roll if the short strike is breached or its delta roughly doubles.
